# Video 2 — STORY REWRITE (supersedes the chapter plan in V2_BRIEF.md; read both)

Why: the first video's real YouTube Studio data showed ~58% retention at 0:30 (normal) but 33% by 1:57, 25.6% average viewed, and a dip near the end of the "chase" chapter. Diagnosis: repeated "look what the power does" scenes, the stakes arrived late, and static wide shots with a small host. So this video is ONE continuous survival journey with a goal ("make it to the next sunrise, hour 100"), and EVERY chapter changes the situation: the host tries a solution, and it creates the next problem.

Narration + timing: `script.json` -> `layout.js` (LAYOUT, ids c1..c8, `vo` line times LOCAL to chapter). Total 304.5 s (5:04). Never hardcode seconds that are not derived from LAYOUT[id].vo[i].start/end: if the VO is regenerated the times move.

## Rules from the review
- The clock is MONOTONIC. No rewinds. H (hours since sunrise) only increases through the video, with explicit time-lapse (clockWhirr, sun/stars wheeling fast, HUD digits racing) wherever it jumps.
- Setting: near the equator, at an equinox (50 h day / 50 h night). The sun moves slowly left->right (east->west).
- Close / medium shots for decisions, reactions and consequences; wide shots only when scale is the information. Mascot must be BIG (s ~ 1.3-2.2 for close and medium) and ALWAYS has an intention, an attempt and a reaction. Never just stand beside narration.
- A visual change should reveal a consequence, decision, result, comparison or scale. Do not add zooms just to fill a 3-5 s slot.
- Only ONE gag per chapter, and only if it doesn't undercut the point.
- Almost no on-screen text: HUD clock + in-world objects (signs, thermometer digits, battery bars, a "YEARS LATER" card is allowed once in c7).
- Hard cuts between chapters are the default. Dips to black are only at c4-in (0.18 s), c7-in (0.3 s), c8-in (0.2 s), configured in full.js (`DIP`). Do not draw your own fades at chapter edges; end each chapter on the action so the cut lands on motion. Each chapter opens in the same shot idea the previous one closed on, where possible (e.g. c4 opens on the same room/curtain line c3 ended on).
- Facts: keep the visual claims no stronger than the narration. Where narration hedges ("likely", "possibly", "could"), do not show certainty (e.g. no giant "70°" thermometer reading as measured air temperature; show a ground-surface thermometer/shimmer and the shoe-sole gag).
- Distinguish the 100-hour day (c1-c6, HUD visible) from the years-long ocean change (c7 after the sunrise). In c7 the HUD stops at 100, fades, and is replaced by a clearly different "years" cue (calendar flipping / globe with a slow time-lapse). NEVER animate the whole sea redistribution as if it happened within the 100 hours.

## Time map (H = hours since sunrise; HUD value)
- c1: H 16 -> 17. Host is exhausted at hour 16 (his body clock says bedtime), noon-ish sky (sun at about 90 degrees elevation: elev = sin(2 pi H/100) = 0.85). Sunset is H 50, so "thirty-four hours" more of sun.
- c2: explanation. HUD hidden (globe/dial/explainer art). Show the rule + the first consequence in the same breath (e.g. the dial 1670 -> ~400 km/h; globe with sun fixed over the host; a tiny host-on-globe cutaway yawning).
- c3: H 17 -> 38 (nap, then a time-lapse to hour 38 at the end of b6).
- c4: H 38 -> 50. d1 about H 40, d2 42, d3 44, d4 46, d5 starts 47 and the sun is dropping by the end.
- c5: H 50 -> 66. n1 sunset at H 50; sleep 50->58 (n6: wakes at H 58, "forty-two more to go" = 100 - 58); stars n7 around H 60-66.
- c6: H 70 -> 86 (w1 "Around hour seventy"; midnight H 75).
- c7: starts at H 100 (sunrise; HUD hits 100 and fades), then years-scale.
- c8: new sunrise light on the coast, HUD gone.

## Chapter plans (L = local seconds; use LAYOUT[id].vo[i].start/end)
**c1 Hook (19 s).** Cold open ON THE CONFLICT, no title, no intro. Close on the host in a sunlit living room/street: heavy eyelids (droop), huge yawn, swaying, rubbing eyes; HUD "HOUR 16". h2: he steps to the window and the sun is blazing high; the sun does a very slow visible crawl (time-lapse hint). h3: HUD pulses, sun big in the sky, quick pull-back to a globe-less simple sun arc timelapse (sunrise->sunset arc stretched, sun arc with 100 ticks). h4: host determined (idea eyes / fist), a sunrise glow far on the horizon with a flag/"finish line" feel (in-world: a sunrise glow, not text).
**c2 The Rule (25.7 s).** r1: globe spinning with a speedometer dial 1670 km/h (GLOBE.dial). r2: dial winds down to ~400 and the globe slows; the host's own sun-over-his-head stays. r4: the day-night split (GLOBE.dayNightSplit) with the host's location pinned on the equator; 50 h bars of day and night (two coloured bars with sun/moon icons, no text). r5: cut to the host in bed, yawning, a little clock in his belly/head ticking at 24-h tempo vs the sun clock — his "body clock" as a small round clock-face over his head. Show consequences, don't just show the globe.
**c3 Body Clock (35 s).** Bedroom: window shows the blazing TOD street (use TOD.drawWorld clipped into a window rect). b1: he hangs blackout curtains, puts on an eye mask, lies down (pose lie, sleepZ). b2: a body-clock dial visible in his head that does not slow: tick tick tick and a sun strip scrolls outside. b3: a cave scene? Keep short: the 1938 Mammoth Cave: a sepia/dimmed cave bunk, a small figure with a lantern and a clock showing 28 hrs while his body-clock dial (24) refuses to follow (dial hand keeps a 24 rhythm). b4: wakes in the dark room, peeks past the curtain at the same sun in the same place, spiral eyes (dizzy). b5: gag: shuffling around with a suitcase and no plane (jet-lag gag). b6: the room's thermometer-style heat shimmer at the window, time-lapse (clockWhirr) 28 -> 38; curtain edges glowing hot, the host pulls the curtain and the sun bleaches the room.
**c4 Long Day (49 s).** Heat and the beach plan. d1: outside, white-hot, host with sunglasses and a towel, sun barely moved; the shadow he casts is short and crisp (TOD.drawCastShadow). d2: desert cutaway: a ground thermometer glued to sand rising to ~70 (label ONLY the Lut-desert sign in-world, with a glowing ground-surface band, no air-temp claim), then a barefoot gag: he steps on pavement and hops (sizzle). d3: the farm: crops wilt (wilt 0 -> 1), thermals rise (heat shimmer columns), storm clouds pile (cloud up). d4: coast: a water thermometer cooler; breezes (windGust lines, palm sway, scarf flutter). d5: he walks east->west hugging a building's shadow; the shadow barely moves (compare: shows a stopwatch/sun trail), he strolls with a cold drink; ends at the beach with the sun lower and orange.
**c5 Long Night (55.7 s).** n1: sunset on the coast; he cheers (sunset relief), then the temperature icon drops. n2: desert + frost (frostGround/frostOverlay), shivering host in a scarf (cold/freezing); do NOT claim freezing is certain: show the ground cooling gradient, the frost only at the end of the line ("possibly"). n3: city skyline lights fail one block at a time (powerDown): a battery bar draining from 100% to 0 in 4 chunks vs a "50 h" bar (in-world, no letters except digits), a solar panel with a moon on it. n4: arrows of power flowing along lines from a sunlit side of the globe to the dark city (a miniature GLOBE cutaway or world strip); some districts go dark. n5: polar animals (penguin? no: use simple silhouettes: owl, deer, fox) improvising; owl awake, the cow confused; skip polar bear if unreadable. n6: he sleeps in a blanket on a bench / sand, snoring, eight hours (HUD H 50->58), wakes, looks up: still dark; shock; "42" shown on the HUD or a tally of 42 ticks. n7: night sky: stars barely move; a time-lapse stack of stars at 4x slower than normal; the host lies on his back, wow-eyes, a satellite or shooting star.
**c6 Weather (40 s).** The storm hits him. w1: night at the coast, palm trees start to sway, the HUD jumps to 70. w2: land-sea breeze diagrams drawn in-world: warm land / cool sea with air loops reversing at night; the host's hood and scarf blow; gag: he clings to a lamp post flag-style. w3: GLOBE.dayNightSplit with a hurricane that fails to wind up as the Coriolis arrow shrinks to 1/4 (use `hurricane` + `tear` options), plus the jet stream bands wobbling. w4: back to the host in the storm, wind howling, holding his hood; light drizzle (rainDrops), end on distant lightning (thunderFar) lighting the sea.
**c7 Oceans (50 s).** o1: sunrise at the beach at H 100 (HUD reaches 100, pops, then slides away); the host cheers/collapses with joy. o2: calendar pages flipping (years) over a time-lapse globe (clear "years" cue). o3: GLOBE.bulgeDiagram with the 21 km bulge. o4: the water in the bulge. o5: the bulge deflates (oblate -> 0) but the rock outline (crust) stays — show the crust as a fixed grey shell while the water slides to the poles; a small clock showing "thousands of years" for rock (hourglass). o6: GLOBE.draw with `sea` animating -> up to ~0.7: equatorial seafloor emerges, polar lowlands flood; end with a final ghost of the old coastline fading in as the rock slowly relaxes. Mark the scale as illustrative: in-world only, no numbers beyond 21. o7: map with new coastlines; the host on a new beach looking at a seafloor appearing.
**c8 Wrap (29 s).** e1: host on a hill looking at a sunrise; question mark pop. e2: three quick medium vignettes of "adaptation": a battery bank/power cable, a shaded farm canopy, a room with warm lamps and curtains (artificial light schedule); a shrug at the end (uncertainty). e3: the familiar 24-hour dial (calm, ticking) next to the sun; the host happily snuggled in bed, thumbs up. e4: like + subscribe: simple in-world buttons (like thumb, bell) with the host pointing, small and tasteful, no long text. e5: "thoughts" teaser: a head silhouette with a bubble storm of soft thought-bubbles (no readable text), `?`. Final 1.4 s fade is applied by full.js; end on a calm frame.

## Sound design principles (for each chapter's cues.json — see sfx_full.mjs and V2_BRIEF)
Quiet, sparse, scene-specific; no whoosh on any chapter boundary or camera move. Let the key consequence land with a short musical rest before it (use `silence` ranges). Ambience beds per scene; music arc per chapter (c1 curious hook; c2 wonder; c3 sleepy/uneasy; c4 hot/tension; c5 cold/lonely -> awe at stars; c6 stormy; c7 awe/scale; c8 warm resolve). 12-35 cues per chapter is plenty; quality over quantity.

## Review expectation
After building, every chapter must be checked by stills at ~1.5 s steps, speed-checked, and logged for: overlapping characters, eye direction, prop contact, tiny action, HUD/narration consistency.

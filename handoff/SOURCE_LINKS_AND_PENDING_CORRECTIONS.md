# Source links and remaining wording checks

Verified links collected during the interrupted continuation on 16 October 2026. These support individual background facts, not a climate simulation of a 100-hour Earth. The existing `HundredHourDay/SOURCES.md` records the wider assumptions and caveats.

- [NASA: The Hottest Spot on Earth](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/the-hottest-spot-on-earth-77627/) — satellite-measured Lut Desert land-surface temperature; distinguish surface from air temperature.
- [NPS: Mammoth Cave Sleep Study](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/dreaming-underground-the-mammoth-cave-sleep-study.htm) — Kleitman and Richardson did not have identical outcomes. Avoid implying every human has an absolute 28-hour adaptation limit.
- [NOAA: The Sea Breeze](https://www.noaa.gov/jetstream/ocean/sea-breeze) — usual day/night reversal and land/water thermal response. This does not establish a particular storm strength under altered rotation.
- [NOAA: Hurricanes](https://www.noaa.gov/education/resource-collections/weather-atmosphere/hurricanes) — background conditions for tropical cyclone formation, including latitude/Coriolis.
- [EIA: Battery storage duration](https://www.eia.gov/TodayinEnergy/detail.php?id=51798) — duration varies by battery use/design; this is historical fleet data, not a universal limit for all current technology.

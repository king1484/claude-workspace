# Sources and numbers — "What If a 1 km Asteroid Hit Your City?"

Assumptions for the scenario: a stony asteroid 1 km across (density ~3,000 kg/m³) hitting at ~20 km/s at a 45° angle into sedimentary rock.

| Narration | Basis |
|---|---|
| "falling at twenty kilometers every second" | Typical asteroid impact speeds are ~17–20 km/s (NASA/JPL CNEOS; Collins, Melosh & Marcus 2005, *Earth Impact Effects Program*, Meteoritics & Planetary Science 40:817). |
| "glows brighter than the sun" | Large entering bodies form a fireball that outshines the Sun for nearby observers; the 20 m Chelyabinsk object (2013) was briefly brighter than the Sun (NASA). |
| "the energy of millions of Hiroshima bombs" | Kinetic energy ½mv²: m ≈ 1.6×10¹² kg, v = 20 km/s → ≈3×10²⁰ J ≈ 75,000 megatons of TNT. Hiroshima ≈ 15 kilotons → ≈5 million. With slower/lighter assumptions (17 km/s, 2,600 kg/m³) it is still ≈3 million. |
| "A fireball over twenty kilometers wide" | Collins et al. 2005, fireball radius R ≈ 0.002·E^(1/3) m → ≈13.6 km radius (≈27 km across). |
| "flattens buildings far beyond the city" | Collins et al. 2005 air-blast model: overpressures that collapse buildings extend tens of km from a ~10⁵ Mt impact. |
| "a crater fifteen kilometers wide" | Collins et al. 2005 crater scaling for the assumptions above → final crater ≈15 km (rule of thumb: 10–20× the impactor). |
| "Rock blasted high into the sky comes raining back down, on fire" | Ballistic ejecta and the vapour plume rise far above the impact site and re-enter the atmosphere hot (Collins et al. 2005; Melosh 1989, *Impact Cratering*). |
| "the dust could dim the sun for the whole planet" | ~1 km impacts are near the threshold for global climate effects from dust and soot (Toon et al. 1997, *Reviews of Geophysics* 35:41; NASA Planetary Defense). Hedged with "could". |
| "NASA has found most asteroids this big, and none are headed our way" | NASA/CNEOS: more than 90% of near-Earth asteroids 1 km or larger have been found, and none of the known ones has a significant chance of hitting Earth for at least the next 100 years. |
| "In 2022, we even crashed a spacecraft into an asteroid, and changed its orbit" | NASA DART hit Dimorphos (≈160 m, orbiting the ≈780 m Didymos about 1.2 km away) on 26 Sep 2022 and shortened its orbit by about 32 minutes. |

Visual simplifications: the city is fictional; timings are compressed for the Short (the real shock wave takes minutes to cross the area); the space shot is not to scale.
